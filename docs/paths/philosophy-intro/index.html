Intro to Philosophy in 7 Days

Philosophy isn't big words in heavy books. It's questions we ask every day without noticing. In 7 days you'll meet 7 ideas that changed how humans think, and you'll try each one out in your own day.

Day 1Socrates

Socrates didn't hand out answers. He kept asking “What do you mean by that?” until people realized they didn't understand what they thought they understood.

Today's task: In your first conversation today, when someone uses a big word like “respectful” or “successful,” calmly ask: “What exactly do you mean by that?”

Quick question: What was Socrates best known for doing?

Day 2Plato

Plato thought what we see around us might be just a shadow of a deeper reality, like people chained in a cave who see only shadows on the wall.

Today's task: Pick something you're very sure of and ask: how do I know it? Did I see it myself, or is it a “shadow” someone described to me?

Quick question: In Plato's cave, what did the chained prisoners see?

Day 3Aristotle

Aristotle said virtue usually sits in the middle between two bad extremes: courage between cowardice and recklessness, generosity between stinginess and wastefulness.

Today's task: Pick one of your traits and think: are you closer to too little, too much, or the middle?

Quick question: For Aristotle, where does courage sit?

Day 4Stoicism

The Stoics split the world in two: things in your control, like your judgments and your reactions, and things outside it, like traffic and other people's opinions.

Today's task: Today, every time you get annoyed, ask: is this in my control or not? Then spend your energy only on what is.

Quick question: Which of these is “in your control,” according to the Stoics?

Day 5René Descartes

Descartes decided to doubt everything he possibly could, to find a solid foundation to build on. He found that the one thing he couldn't doubt was that he was thinking, and therefore he existed.

Today's task: Pick something you heard today and doubt it for real: what could make it wrong?

Quick question: What did Descartes find he could not doubt?

Day 6Immanuel Kant

Kant proposed a test for any action: if everyone did what you're doing, would the world still work? He also said people must be treated as ends in themselves, never merely as tools.

Today's task: Before doing something “small” today, like cutting in line, ask: what would happen if everyone did this?

Quick question: What does Kant's test for any action ask?

Day 7Existentialism

The existentialists said humans are born without a ready-made meaning, and we create ourselves through our choices. Huge freedom, and even bigger responsibility.

Today's task: Write down one choice you made today, and under it: what does this choice say about the person I want to be?

Quick question: For the existentialists, where does the meaning of your life come from?
