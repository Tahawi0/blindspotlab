Social Media Without Getting Sucked In

Apps are designed to keep you scrolling. In 7 days you'll learn the tricks that hook you and the mistakes your brain makes while you scroll, and you'll be the one holding the phone again.

Day 1Survivorship Bias

You only see the people who made it: the one who quit their job, started a business, and got rich. The one who quit and went broke doesn't make a TikTok about it.

Today's task: If you see a success story today, ask: where are the people who did the same thing and didn't make it?

Quick question: “Everyone who dropped out of college to start a company succeeded.” What's wrong with that?

Day 2Spotlight Effect

You think everyone noticed the post you deleted or the unflattering photo. In reality, everyone is busy thinking about themselves.

Today's task: Think of a post or comment you once worried about. Does anyone still remember it? Do you remember other people's posts from last month?

Quick question: What does the spotlight effect mean?

Day 3Bandwagon Effect

A million likes doesn't mean something is true. It means a million people tapped a button.

Today's task: Before you share something today, cover the like count with your thumb and ask: if this post had only 3 likes, would I believe it?

Quick question: A post has 100,000 shares. What does that prove?

Day 4Barnum Effect

“You're a sensitive person but you hide it, and you have untapped potential.” That fits almost anyone, which is why personality quizzes and horoscopes feel so accurate.

Today's task: If you come across a “What's your personality type?” quiz today, read the result to someone next to you and ask: does this fit you?

Quick question: Why do online personality quiz results feel so accurate?

Day 5Negativity Bias

Upsetting posts and scary headlines grab your brain more. And the apps notice when you stop on them, so they show you more.

Today's task: After 15 minutes of scrolling today, ask yourself: how do I feel right now? Better or worse than before I opened the app?

Quick question: Why do outrage posts spread more?

Day 6Groupthink

In a group, when everyone agrees, nobody wants to be the different voice. So the whole group can follow a bad idea without anyone objecting.

Today's task: In one of your group chats, think about the last time someone disagreed with the majority. What happened to them? And if you disagreed and kept quiet, why?

Quick question: When does groupthink happen?

Day 7Moving the Goalposts

You bring the evidence they asked for, and they say, “No, I want something else.” It happens all the time in comment sections, and it means the argument never ends.

Today's task: Before you jump into a comment-section debate today, ask the other person: “What evidence would change your mind?” If the answer is nothing, save your time.

Quick question: You send them the study they asked for, and they say, “That's not enough, I want 10.” What's this called?
