7 Days to Read the News Without Panicking

News stories and posts scare and generalize more than reality does. In 7 days you'll learn to read any story with a calm head.

Day 1Availability Heuristic

If the news talks about something a lot, it feels like it happens a lot. But most talked-about isn't the same as most common.

Today's task: Pick a story that scared you today and look for a number: how often does this actually happen?

Quick question: After a week of shark attack headlines, you're nervous about swimming at the beach. What's the first question to ask?

Day 2Negativity Bias

Bad news grabs your attention far more than good news. That's not a flaw in you. It's a brain that evolved to survive.

Today's task: Write down 3 good things that happened in the news today. You'll find it surprisingly hard, and that's the lesson.

Quick question: A news site notices that scary headlines get far more clicks. What is it exploiting?

Day 3Anecdotal Evidence

“My cousin had this happen” is a story, not a statistic. Stories move us, but they don't prove anything.

Today's task: When you hear a story used as evidence today, ask: and how many people did this not happen to?

Quick question: A post says: “My neighbor took this new cold medicine and felt sick for a week, so it's dangerous.” What's the problem?

Day 4Hasty Generalization

One video of one incident says nothing about an entire group of people or a whole profession.

Today's task: Find a post that generalizes from a single case, and write a polite reply to it.

Quick question: A video of a delivery driver yelling at a customer goes viral, and the comments say, “All delivery drivers are like this.” What's this called?

Day 5False Cause (Post Hoc)

Two things happening one after the other doesn't mean one caused the other.

Today's task: Find a headline with “after” or “because of” in it, and ask: could a third factor be behind both?

Quick question: Headline: “Break-ins rose in the neighborhood after the new mall opened.” Before you believe the mall caused it, what do you ask?

Day 6Cherry Picking

Anyone can pick the numbers that suit them and leave out the rest. The question is: where's the rest of the picture?

Today's task: On any chart you see today, look at the time range it shows. If you zoomed out, would the picture change?

Quick question: A chart shows a price plunging, but it only covers the last 3 days. What's the right question?

Day 7Confirmation Bias

We believe stories that agree with us without checking them. It's the most dangerous bias when it comes to reading the news.

Today's task: Today, read one article by someone you disagree with, all the way to the end, without replying.

Quick question: A story lands in the family group chat that backs up your opinion, so you forward it to every group you're in without checking. What's most likely behind that?
