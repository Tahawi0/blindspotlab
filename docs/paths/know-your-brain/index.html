Know Your Brain

Your brain is fast and clever, but it takes shortcuts that trip it up in predictable ways. In 7 days you'll meet 7 of them and start catching them as they happen.

Day 1Confirmation Bias

Your brain looks for whatever confirms what you already believe, and slides right past whatever contradicts it.

Today's task: Pick an opinion you're sure about, find the strongest argument against it today, and read it all the way through without replying.

Quick question: What does confirmation bias mean?

Day 2Availability Heuristic

Whatever gets talked about a lot and sticks in your head feels common, even when it's rare.

Today's task: Pick something you're afraid of and look up a real number: how often does it actually happen?

Quick question: Why are so many people more afraid of flying than driving?

Day 3Base Rate Neglect

When you hear a description of someone, you forget to ask: how many people like that are there in the first place? The description grabs your attention, and the base rates get forgotten.

Today's task: Today, when you judge someone or something by how it “looks,” ask: out of everyone, how many does this actually describe?

Quick question: Someone is quiet and loves books. Are they more likely a librarian or a salesperson?

Day 4Anchoring

The first number you hear becomes an anchor, and every estimate after it circles around it.

Today's task: Before you ask the price of something today, guess it yourself first and write it down.

Quick question: The salesperson says, “This is $200, but I'll do it for you at $120.” What role is the $200 playing?

Day 5Hindsight Bias

After something happens, you feel like you “knew it all along.” But that's your brain rewriting the memory.

Today's task: Write down one prediction today about something that will happen this week. Keep the note and check it at the end of the week.

Quick question: Saying “I knew that would happen” after the game is usually a sign of what?

Day 6Fundamental Attribution Error

When someone else messes up, you think “they're careless.” When you mess up, you think “circumstances.” That's the fundamental attribution error.

Today's task: The next time someone annoys you today, look for one thing about their situation that could explain what they did.

Quick question: A driver cuts you off. Which thought pushes back against the attribution error?

Day 7Self-Serving Bias

We credit our successes to our own skill and blame our failures on luck and other people. It protects how we feel about ourselves, but it keeps us from learning.

Today's task: Think of your last success and your last failure. For each, write one cause that came from you and one from outside.

Quick question: A student who passed says “I'm smart,” and when he fails he says “The test was unfair.” What's this called?
