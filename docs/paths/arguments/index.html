7 Days to Win Arguments Without Being a Jerk

A discussion isn't a fight. In 7 days you'll learn to spot the tricks that wreck any conversation, and answer them without raising your voice.

Day 1Straw Man

Step one in any discussion: make sure you've understood the other person correctly before you respond.

Today's task: In your first disagreement today, say “So what you're saying is…?” before you reply.

Quick question: You said “Tests need more questions that check understanding,” and someone replied, “So you want to get rid of tests?!” What's the best response?

Day 2Ad Hominem

When someone attacks you instead of answering your point, it usually means they don't have an answer.

Today's task: Scroll through the comments on any post and notice how many replies attack the person instead of the idea.

Quick question: In a discussion about traffic, someone replies: “You don't even own a car, so why are you talking?” What's going on in that reply?

Day 3False Dilemma

“You're either with me or against me.” There are almost always more than two options.

Today's task: When someone boxes you into two choices, look for a third.

Quick question: “Either you study 10 hours a day, or you fail.” What's wrong with this?

Day 4Whataboutism

“Well, what about so-and-so?” moves the conversation somewhere else. A good reply: “Fair, let's talk about that later. Let's finish this first.”

Today's task: Try that reply once today, or notice who uses this move.

Quick question: You tell your manager your team's projects are running late, and they reply, “Well, isn't the sales team even further behind?” What is this move called?

Day 5Shifting the Burden of Proof

Whoever makes the claim has to bring the evidence. It's not your job to prove them wrong.

Today's task: When someone says “Prove me wrong,” answer: “You made the claim. Show me your evidence.”

Quick question: Your friend says, “Green tea cures diabetes, and you can't prove it doesn't.” Who has to provide the evidence?

Day 6Tu Quoque

“Well, you do it too” might be true, but it doesn't make the point itself wrong.

Today's task: Think of advice you rejected because the person giving it didn't follow it. Was the advice itself right?

Quick question: Your dad says “Quit smoking,” and you answer, “But you smoke!” What does that change about the advice?

Day 7Fallacy Fallacy

The last lesson is the most important: someone using a fallacy doesn't mean their view is wrong. They could be right for a bad reason.

Today's task: In a discussion today, instead of saying “That's a fallacy,” look for the strongest argument that could support the other side.

Quick question: Someone says, “We should drink plenty of water, because my mom said so.” That's a weak argument. What does that mean?
