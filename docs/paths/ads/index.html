7 Days to Stop Getting Played by Ads

Every ad tries to stir something in you before you think. In 7 days you'll learn the 7 most common tricks, and you'll start seeing them everywhere.

Day 1Anchoring

“Was $90, now $39.” That first number is there to be an anchor, so everything after it looks cheap.

Today's task: Today, the first time you see a discount, ask: if I'd never seen the old price, would $39 still look cheap?

Quick question: A phone is labeled “Was $1,499, now $899.” Which question breaks the spell of the first number?

Day 2Decoy Effect

The medium popcorn at the movies isn't really there to be sold. It's there to make the large look like a deal.

Today's task: Find a price menu with three options and try to figure out which one is the decoy.

Quick question: Gym membership: 1 month for $50, 6 months for $290, or 1 year for $300. Why is the 6-month option probably there?

Day 3Bandwagon Effect

“Best seller” and “a million people tried it” don't tell you the product is good. They tell you a lot of people bought it.

Today's task: Today, count how many ads use the number of customers instead of telling you what the product actually does.

Quick question: An ad says “Over a million customers chose us.” What does that sentence prove?

Day 4Authority Bias

The white coat in a commercial doesn't necessarily belong to a doctor. And even if it does, that person is being paid.

Today's task: When you see an expert in an ad, ask: are they an expert in this exact thing? And are they being paid to say it?

Quick question: A toothpaste ad shows someone in a white coat saying “I recommend it.” What should you think first?

Day 5Framing Effect

“95% fat-free” and “contains 5% fat” are the exact same fact. The first one just sells better.

Today's task: Take a line from an ad and rewrite it the opposite way. Is it still convincing?

Quick question: One yogurt says “97% sugar-free.” Another says “contains 3% sugar.” What's the difference?

Day 6Loss Aversion

“Offer ends tonight.” “Don't miss out.” Ads scare you about losing, because a loss stings more than a gain feels good.

Today's task: If you feel like you have to buy something right now today, wait 24 hours and see whether the feeling sticks around.

Quick question: You get a text: “Your reward points expire tomorrow unless you make a purchase today.” What is this playing on?

Day 7Halo Effect

The actor you love isn't an expert on cooking oil or banking. But your fondness for them rubs off on the product.

Today's task: Think of the last thing you bought because of someone famous. Would you have bought it if a regular person had been in the ad?

Quick question: A famous NFL star appears in ads for an investing company. Why do so many people find the ad convincing?
