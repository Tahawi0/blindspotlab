Thinking Tools

The Atlas teaches you to spot the mistake. These tools help you think it through: small questions and rules to use before you decide, believe, or reply.

  • Occam's RazorThinking ToolIf you have more than one explanation for the same thing, start with the one that needs the fewest assumptions.
  • Hanlon's RazorThinking ToolDon’t explain someone’s behavior with bad intent if a slip, a mistake, or simply not knowing is enough to explain it.
  • SteelmanningThinking ToolPresent the other person’s view in its strongest, best form, and only then respond to it.
  • First-Principles ThinkingThinking ToolBreak a problem down to the basic facts you’re sure of, and build the solution from there instead of copying “what everyone usually does.”
  • InversionThinking ToolInstead of asking “What should I do to succeed?” ask “What would guarantee I fail?” and steer clear of it.
  • Second-Order ThinkingThinking ToolDon’t stop at the first consequence of a decision. Ask: “And then what?”
  • Chesterton's FenceThinking ToolDon’t remove a rule or an old practice until you first know why it was put there.
  • Bayesian UpdatingThinking ToolHold your view with a level of confidence, and raise or lower it based on how strong each new piece of evidence is, instead of all-or-nothing.
  • Falsifiability TestThinking ToolDecide up front what evidence would make you admit you’re wrong.
  • PremortemThinking ToolBefore you start a plan, imagine it has already failed, and ask: “Why did it fail?”

Every tool has limits, and they're listed on its page. A tool helps you think. It doesn't think for you.
