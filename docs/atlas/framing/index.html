Bias

Framing Effect

The exact same information can lead us to different decisions depending on how it's worded.

Where you'll meet it: Shopping and money Health and food

In real life

At the doctor's office
“This surgery has a 90% success rate” sounds more reassuring than “10% of these surgeries fail,” even though it's the same number.
At the grocery store
Ground beef labeled “95% lean” sells better than the same beef labeled “5% fat.”
On a used-car lot
“Just $15 a day” sounds lighter than “$450 a month.”

How to spot it

Flip the wording in your head: if the same thing were said the opposite way, would I feel the same about it?

How to respond without a fight

Convert everything into full numbers in the same format, then compare.

Don't confuse it with: Equivocation

Equivocation changes what a word means. Framing leaves the meaning the same but presents it in a way that nudges you.

Strength of evidence

Strong

Test yourself

"This medication works in 80% of cases" makes you feel good about it. When the doctor says "20% of patients don't improve," you get nervous.

What's the trick here?

Both numbers say exactly the same thing. Only the wording changed how you felt.

Sources

  • Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1981). The framing of decisions and the psychology of choice. Science, 211(4481), 453–458.
