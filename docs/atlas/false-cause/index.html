Fallacy

False Cause (Post Hoc)

Post Hoc / False Cause

You assume one thing caused another just because it happened before it, or alongside it.

Where you'll meet it: Home and family Work and school Online and in the news

In real life

At home
“I wore this jersey and my team won, so it’s my lucky jersey.”
On social media
“Ever since they put up that cell tower, people around here keep getting sick. It has to be the tower.”
At work
“Sales went up after we changed the logo color.” — Maybe it was the holiday season, or a promotion.

How to spot it

Ask: could a third factor be causing both? And does this happen every time, or was it a coincidence?

How to respond without a fight

“Maybe. But it could also be this other thing. How would we figure out which one is the real cause?”

Don't confuse it with: Correlation isn’t causation

When two things keep happening together, that’s a correlation. It still doesn’t mean one caused the other — you need more evidence for that.

Test yourself

"Ever since the new neighbors moved in, our Wi-Fi keeps dropping. They're bad luck."

What's the trick here?

Two things happening at the same time doesn't mean one caused the other. Outages have real causes in the network itself.

Sources

  • Hume, D. (1748). An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding.
  • Pearl, J., & Mackenzie, D. (2018). The Book of Why. Basic Books.
