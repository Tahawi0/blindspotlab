Fallacy

False Equivalence

Treating two things as equal because they share one feature, even though they differ hugely in size or seriousness.

Where you'll meet it: Home and family Online and in the news Sports and out and about

In real life

At home
“You forgot to pick up milk, and I forgot to pay the rent. So we're even.”
In the locker room
“The guy who showed up five minutes late to practice is the same as the guy who shoved the ref. They both broke the rules.”
On the local news
A segment treats a long wait at an urgent care clinic and a ring selling counterfeit pills as “the same problem.”

How to spot it

Ask: are these two things really equal, or do they just share one trait? Where's the difference in size and harm?

How to respond without a fight

“Sure, both are wrong. But a $5 mistake isn't a $5,000 mistake. Let's keep each one in proportion.”

Don't confuse it with: False Analogy

A false analogy uses a resemblance between two things to reach a new conclusion. A false equivalence claims the two things carry the same weight in the first place.

Sources

  • Damer, T. E. (2009). Attacking Faulty Reasoning: A Practical Guide to Fallacy-Free Arguments (6th ed.). Wadsworth Cengage Learning.
  • Bennett, B. (2017). Logically Fallacious: The Ultimate Collection of Over 300 Logical Fallacies (Academic ed.). eBookIt.
