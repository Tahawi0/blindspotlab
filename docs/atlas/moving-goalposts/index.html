Fallacy

Moving the Goalposts

Setting a condition for being convinced, then changing it to something harder once it's met, instead of admitting the point.

Where you'll meet it: Home and family Work and school Online and in the news

In real life

At home
“Get straight A's and you can have a new phone.” — He gets straight A's. — “No, that was just the first semester. Wait till the end of the year.”
On Reddit
“Show me proof.” — Here's a study. — “That one's tiny.” — Here's a big one. — “Somebody must have paid for it.”
At work
“If the app gets 100 sign-ups in a month, we keep funding it.” — It gets 120. — “No, we really needed 500 for it to count as a success.”

How to spot it

Remember the condition that was set at the start. If every time it's met a new, harder one appears, the conclusion was probably decided before the evidence.

How to respond without a fight

Agree on the condition up front and write it down: “What exactly would change your mind?” If it's met, calmly point back to it.

Don't confuse it with: No True Scotsman

No True Scotsman changes the definition to push the counterexample out. Moving the goalposts raises the bar for evidence every time the evidence clears it.

Sources

  • Damer, T. E. (2009). Attacking Faulty Reasoning: A Practical Guide to Fallacy-Free Arguments (6th ed.). Wadsworth Cengage Learning.
  • Bennett, B. (2017). Logically Fallacious: The Ultimate Collection of Over 300 Logical Fallacies (Academic ed.). eBookIt.
