Fallacy

Sunk Cost Fallacy

Sticking with a losing bet because of what you've already put in, not because it's worth continuing.

Where you'll meet it: Work and school Shopping and money Sports and out and about

In real life

At the movies
“We paid $16 a ticket. We're sitting through this terrible movie to the end.”
At the mechanic
“I've already sunk $5,000 into repairs on this car, so I have to fix it again.” — Even if the repair now costs more than the car is worth.
At work
“We've spent a year on this project. We can't stop now.” — Even when it's clearly not going to work.

How to spot it

Ask: if I hadn't spent anything yet, would I start this today? What's spent is gone. The only question is what comes next.

How to respond without a fight

“That money's gone whether we keep going or stop. The question is: what's the best move from here?”

Don't confuse it with: Commitment and persistence

Pushing through something hard because it's likely to pay off is grit. The fallacy is pushing on only because of the past, when the future isn't on your side.

Test yourself

"I've spent two years in this major and I can't stand it. But I can't switch now, or those two years were wasted."

What's the trick here?

Those two years are gone either way, whether you stay or switch. The real question: which choice is better for the years ahead?

Sources

  • Arkes, H. R., & Blumer, C. (1985). The psychology of sunk cost. Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 35(1), 124–140.
