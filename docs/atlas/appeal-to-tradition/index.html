Fallacy

Appeal to Tradition

You treat something as right because “we’ve always done it this way.”

Where you'll meet it: Home and family Work and school Sports and out and about

In real life

At Thanksgiving
“We’ve always bundled babies up in heavy blankets, even in July. You think you know better than Grandma?”
At work
“We’ve done this spreadsheet by hand for ten years. Why change it now?”
In the community
“A wedding has to be done this way. That’s just how people do it.”

How to spot it

If the only reason is “we’ve always done it” or “that’s just how it’s done,” nobody has said yet why it’s actually good.

How to respond without a fight

“It might be a great tradition. But what does it actually do for us? And if we find a better way, can we try it?”

Don't confuse it with: Respecting accumulated experience

Traditions are sometimes the distilled result of real trial and error, and that’s a good reason to take them seriously. The fallacy is treating age alone as the proof.

Test yourself

"Why would we use budgeting software? My dad and grandpa kept a paper checkbook register their whole lives and it worked fine."

What's the trick here?

The only reason given is "that's how it was always done." Nobody explained why the paper register is actually better.

Sources

  • Tindale, C. (2007). Fallacies and Argument Appraisal. Cambridge University Press.
