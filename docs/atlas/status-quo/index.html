Bias

Status Quo Bias

Preferring that things stay the way they are, even when a change would be better for us.

Where you'll meet it: Home and family Work and school Shopping and money

In real life

At home
Your phone plan is overpriced and doesn't fit how you use it, but you keep it because you're “used to it.”
At work
A new tool would cut the team's busywork in half, but nobody wants to try it.
With your money
Your savings have sat for years in an account that pays next to nothing, because switching feels like a hassle.

How to spot it

Ask: if I were choosing from scratch today, would I pick this?

How to respond without a fight

Put a reminder on your calendar every few months to review the things you never revisit, like plans and streaming subscriptions.

Don't confuse it with: Appeal to Tradition

Appeal to tradition is a fallacy that says “it's right because it's always been done this way.” Status quo bias is an inner pull to avoid change, even without any argument.

Strength of evidence

Strong

Test yourself

You're offered a better phone than yours at no extra cost, and you turn it down because "I'm used to mine."

What's the trick here?

The new option is better. It's the change itself that bothers you.

Sources

  • Samuelson, W., & Zeckhauser, R. (1988). Status quo bias in decision making. Journal of Risk and Uncertainty, 1(1), 7–59.
