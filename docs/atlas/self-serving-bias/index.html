Bias

Self-Serving Bias

When we succeed, it’s because we’re good. When we fail, it’s because of the circumstances or other people.

Where you'll meet it: Work and school Sports and out and about

In real life

At school
“I aced it because I studied hard.” And in the class you failed: “The professor is unfair and the exam was impossible.”
In sports
When your team wins: “We played great.” When it loses: “The refs robbed us.”
At work
When the deal closes: “I closed it.” When it falls through: “Accounting held up the paperwork.”

How to spot it

Compare how you explain your wins with how you explain your losses. If success always comes from you and failure always comes from outside, there’s a bias at work.

How to respond without a fight

Privately, after any failure ask, “What part of this was in my control?” And after any success ask, “What outside help did I get?”

Don't confuse it with: Fundamental Attribution Error

The fundamental attribution error is about explaining other people’s behavior by their character. Self-serving bias is about how we explain our own successes and failures.

Strength of evidence

Strong, though its size varies from culture to culture

Sources

  • Miller, D. T., & Ross, M. (1975). Self-serving biases in the attribution of causality: Fact or fiction? Psychological Bulletin, 82(2), 213–225.
  • Mezulis, A. H., Abramson, L. Y., Hyde, J. S., & Hankin, B. L. (2004). Is there a universal positivity bias in attributions? A meta-analytic review of individual, developmental, and cultural differences in the self-serving attributional bias. Psychological Bulletin, 130(5), 711–747.
