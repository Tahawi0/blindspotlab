Philosopher

René Descartes

France, the Netherlands and Sweden, 1596–1650

He decided to doubt everything he possibly could, and found one thing he couldn't doubt: that he was thinking.

The idea

Descartes wanted a rock-solid foundation for knowledge. So he said: I'll doubt my senses, because they fool me. I'll doubt that I'm awake, because I might be dreaming. I'll even doubt arithmetic. But there's one thing I can't doubt: that someone is doing the doubting. That's where his famous line comes from: “I think, therefore I am.” He also gave us the coordinate system you used in algebra class, which is why it's called the Cartesian plane.

In real life

You wake up from a very realistic dream and need a few seconds to be sure you're actually awake. That's the same question Descartes started with: how do I know this isn't a dream right now?

Use it today

When you're facing a big decision, try his approach: write down everything you think you know, then cross out anything that could be wrong. What's left is your foundation.

The criticism

His split between mind and body as two separate substances has been heavily criticized, because it doesn't explain how the two affect each other. And some argue that the “I” in “I think” is itself an assumption.
