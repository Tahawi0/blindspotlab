Absurdism

France, 20th century

We search for meaning, and the universe stays silent. The answer isn't despair; it's to live fully awake anyway.

The idea

Albert Camus said “the absurd” is the clash between our hunger for meaning and the silence of the universe. He saw three possible responses: escape from life itself, escape into a ready-made answer without thinking it through, or rebel and live with passion despite the absurd. Camus chose the third. His example was Sisyphus, condemned to push a boulder uphill forever, and he concluded: “One must imagine Sisyphus happy.”

In real life

Same commute, same cubicle, same small talk every day. Camus wouldn't tell you to quit. He'd tell you that you can choose to see each day with fresh eyes, and that choice is itself a kind of rebellion.

Use it today

In the most routine moment of your day, find one thing to genuinely enjoy: your coffee, the light through the window, a kind word.

The criticism

Some people believe the universe isn't silent at all, and that religion or philosophy offers real answers. Others feel Camus's “revolt” is more poetic than practical.
