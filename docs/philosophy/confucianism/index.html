School of Thought

Confucianism

China, from the 6th and 5th centuries BCE

A good society starts with good people, and good people are built through ethics and courtesy in everyday dealings.

The idea

Confucius was a teacher in ancient China, and his students collected his sayings after his death in the Analects. For him, ethics is learned in everyday relationships: child and parent, student and teacher, ruler and people. Two key ideas are ren, meaning humaneness or kindness, and li, the rituals and manners that structure relationships. He's credited with the rule: “What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others.” Later, Mencius argued that human nature is basically good, while Xunzi argued it leans toward bad and is corrected through education and ritual.

In real life

You get promoted and now you manage your former coworkers. Confucius talked about “the rectification of names”: if your title is “manager,” act like a real manager. Be fair and protect your team. Trouble starts when the title says one thing and the behavior says another.

Use it today

Pick one relationship, with your kid, a coworker or a neighbor, and ask: what does this role ask of me? Then start with something small: a hello, a question, a thank-you.

The criticism

It can be used to justify obeying elders and authority without question, and it placed women in a lower position. The Chinese philosopher Mozi objected that care should go to all people equally, not to those closest to us first.
