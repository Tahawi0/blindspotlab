Philosopher

Simone de Beauvoir

Paris, 1908–1986

She said one is not born a woman but becomes one: society is what writes much of the role.

The idea

Beauvoir was a French existentialist philosopher and novelist. In The Second Sex (1949) she wrote her famous line: “One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman.” She meant that much of what gets called “women's nature” is really upbringing and social expectation. She argued that society treats man as the default and woman as “the Other,” defined in relation to him. In The Ethics of Ambiguity she argued that my freedom is tied to other people's freedom: I can't be truly free while I'm limiting someone else's.

In real life

“Girls just naturally like cooking.” Beauvoir would ask: if it's natural, why do we have to keep teaching it and reminding them? And why are so many of the most famous restaurant chefs men?

Use it today

When you hear a sentence that starts with “women are naturally…” or “men are naturally…,” ask: is this actually biology, or something we learned and got used to?

The criticism

Later feminist thinkers said she generalized the experience of middle-class European women to all women. Others felt she viewed motherhood and the female body too negatively.
