School of Thought

Existentialism

Europe, 19th and 20th centuries

Nobody is going to hand you a ready-made meaning for your life. You're free, and you're responsible for what you do with that freedom.

The idea

It began with Kierkegaard and Nietzsche and became famous with Jean-Paul Sartre and Simone de Beauvoir in France. Sartre summed it up as “existence precedes essence”: a paper knife is designed for a specific purpose before it's ever made, but a human being exists first and then defines who they are through their choices. That freedom brings anxiety, because you can't hand the responsibility to anyone else.

In real life

“My parents picked my major.” An existentialist would say: okay, but you chose to stay in it for four years, and you're the one who'll choose what to do after graduation.

Use it today

Notice how many times today you say “I had no choice.” There usually was a choice. It was just a hard one.

The criticism

Some feel it underestimates the real limits of poverty, circumstance and society, as if everyone had the same amount of freedom. Beauvoir herself wrote about how circumstances shape a woman's freedom differently.
