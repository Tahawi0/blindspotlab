School of Thought

Phenomenology

Germany and France, from the early 20th century

Before you ask “What is this thing really?”, describe carefully how it shows up for you.

The idea

Its founder was Edmund Husserl, and its famous slogan is “back to the things themselves.” Husserl built on his teacher Franz Brentano's idea that consciousness is always consciousness of something: you think about something, you're afraid of something. He proposed setting aside, for the moment, the question “Does the world really exist the way we see it?” and focusing on describing experience as we live it. He called this “bracketing.” After him, Heidegger, Sartre and Maurice Merleau-Ponty took the idea in new directions, and Merleau-Ponty focused on the body as our basic way of knowing the world.

In real life

The same hour: waiting at the doctor's office for test results, it feels endless; hanging out with friends at a coffee shop, it's gone in a second. The clock on the wall is the same, but the time you live through is different, and a phenomenologist thinks that difference deserves serious study.

Use it today

When you're upset, before you judge (“I'm a failure,” “he did it on purpose”), just write a description: What do I feel in my body? What exactly did I see and hear?

The criticism

How can we know your description of your experience is accurate, or compare it with someone else's? Descriptions from the inside are hard to double-check the way an experiment is. And even Heidegger argued that we're always already inside the world, so we can't bracket it and stand outside watching.
