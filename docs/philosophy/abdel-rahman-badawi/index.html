Philosopher

Abdel Rahman Badawi

Damietta, Cairo and Paris, 1917–2002

The Egyptian philosopher who brought existentialism into Arabic, and believed every person is a unique existence, not a number in a crowd.

The idea

Badawi was born in a village in Egypt's Nile Delta and studied philosophy at Fuad I University (today Cairo University). His 1944 doctoral thesis, Existential Time, was strongly influenced by Heidegger. His idea was that a human being isn't a fixed object like a chair; a person is an existence that unfolds in time, through choices, anxiety and freedom. What matters is the individual, not “what people say.” Beyond existentialism, he wrote, edited and translated an enormous number of books, including medieval Arabic translations of ancient Greek texts, and books on Nietzsche and others. He lived for many years in Paris and returned to Cairo a few months before his death in 2002.

In real life

Someone squeezed into a packed subway car at rush hour in New York feels like nobody, one copy out of millions. Badawi would tell him: the crowd is what makes you forget you're an individual. You're not “one of the people.” You're the only one who will ever live this life of yours.

Use it today

Count how many decisions you made this week because “that's what everyone does.” Pick one and ask yourself: if no one were watching, what would I have done?

The criticism

Critics said his existentialism was mostly Heidegger rendered into Arabic, rather than a philosophy growing out of Egyptian life. His style in these books is also dense and abstract, so they reached only a small circle of specialists.
