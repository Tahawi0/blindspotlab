Analyze It

Philosopher

David Hume

Scotland, 1711–1776

He pointed out that the sun rising every day so far isn't logical proof it will rise tomorrow, and that we live by habit more than by reason.

The idea

Hume was an empiricist: all our knowledge comes from experience. But when he looked closely at experience, he found a problem. We see things happen one after another, but we never see the “cause” itself. Expecting the future to be like the past is habit, not logic. This is called the problem of induction. He also wrote that reason is “the slave of the passions,” meaning we decide with our feelings and then reason shows up with the justifications.

In real life

A turkey gets fed every morning at 9 a.m. Day after day, it concludes that food arrives at 9 a.m. Then comes the week before Thanksgiving. The example came after Hume, but it captures his point perfectly.

Use it today

When you catch yourself saying “That's never happened, so it won't happen,” remember the turkey. The past is a clue, not a guarantee.

The criticism

Kant said Hume woke him from his “dogmatic slumber,” and spent years trying to answer him. Philosophers of science like Karl Popper argued that science doesn't need to solve the problem of induction at all: science tests ideas and tries to prove them false.
