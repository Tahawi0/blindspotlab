Determinism and Free Will

An old debate, from the Stoics to today's neuroscience

If everything has a cause before it, where is there room for your choice? And can we still hold anyone responsible?

The idea

This is a debate between three camps. Hard determinists: every event is the result of earlier causes, so your choice was already settled and freedom is an illusion. In 1814 Pierre-Simon Laplace imagined an intellect that knew the position of every particle and could calculate the entire future. Libertarians, in the metaphysical sense, not the political one: a person really could have chosen differently. And compatibilists, like Hume: freedom means acting on your own reasoning and desires without being forced, even if everything has a cause. In surveys of academic philosophers, compatibilism is the most common view.

In real life

Your coworker blows up in a meeting and says, “That's just me, I'm hot-headed.” A hard determinist: that's the product of his upbringing and his genes. A compatibilist: nobody was holding his arm, and he can learn to calm down, so he's responsible. A libertarian: he had a real choice to stay quiet.

Use it today

When you say “I couldn't help it,” separate the causes that pushed you from the fact that you can still learn and change. Causes explain; they don't automatically excuse.

The criticism

Each camp faces a hard question. Hard determinists: without freedom, do praise and blame mean anything? Libertarians: if a choice doesn't come from causes, it's random, and randomness isn't freedom. Compatibilists are accused of redefining “freedom” just to make the problem go away.
