School of Thought

Renaissance Humanism

Italy, then Europe, 14th to 16th centuries

Go back to the original sources, and learn to write, speak and think well, because education is what shapes a person.

The idea

During the Renaissance, scholars in Italy, Petrarch the best known among them, went back to reading ancient Greek and Latin authors like Cicero and Seneca. Their motto was ad fontes, “back to the sources”: don't rely on summaries that have passed through a hundred hands; read the original text. They championed the study of grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry and moral philosophy. In the Low Countries, Erasmus collected and explained thousands of ancient proverbs, and in 1511 published The Praise of Folly, a satire of vanity and pedantry.

In real life

A post with “As the great philosopher once said…” and a million shares. A Renaissance humanist would ask: where exactly is that in his writings? Chances are the quote has been changed along the way, or made up entirely.

Use it today

Pick a quote or a statistic you hear a lot, and track down the original source yourself once this week. You'll be surprised how much things change along the way.

The criticism

It was an education for an elite who knew Latin and had money. Some of its followers revered the ancients so much that they copied them word for word, so much so that Erasmus himself mocked them in a book in 1528. And their focus on literature made them, at first, less interested in the sciences.
