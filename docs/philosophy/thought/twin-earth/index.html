Thought Experiments

Twin Earth

Hilary Putnam, “The Meaning of ‘Meaning,’” 1975

Imagine a faraway planet so much like Earth that there's a copy of you on it. Same looks, same memories, same brain, atom for atom. The only difference: the liquid in the oceans and faucets there, which people call “water,” isn't H2O. It's a completely different chemical that looks and tastes exactly like water. Now go back to 1750, before anyone knew what water is made of. You and your twin both say, “I'd like a glass of water,” with exactly the same picture in your heads.

Does the word “water” mean the same thing when you and your twin say it?

What did philosophers say?

Hilary Putnam
He said no: you mean H2O, and your twin means the other stuff, even though what's in your heads is identical. So meaning isn't all inside the head. Part of it is fixed by the world around you. His famous line: “Meanings just ain't in the head.” He added that you can say “gold” without being able to tell it from fake gold, because you rely on experts.
Tyler Burge
In 1979, he extended the idea: not just nature, but society too helps fix what your words and thoughts mean. A man complains to his doctor that he has “arthritis” in his thigh, even though arthritis affects only the joints. What his words mean is set by medical usage in his community, even though he has it wrong.
The internalists (like Jerry Fodor)
They hold that part of meaning, which they call “narrow content,” is shared by you and your twin. That part is what explains your behavior: you both drink when you're thirsty and go looking for the same thing. So we shouldn't say you think differently just because the world outside is different.

Why it matters today

You say “I've got the flu” and mean one thing, and your doctor hears another. You buy “probiotics” without knowing exactly what they are, trusting the pharmacist or the label. Putnam reminds you that your words are tied to the world and to the people around you, and that sometimes a disagreement isn't about ideas at all, but about what the word is actually pointing to.
