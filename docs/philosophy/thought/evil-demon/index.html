Descartes' Evil Demon

René Descartes, Meditations on First Philosophy, 1641

You're sitting on your porch, watching the street, hearing car horns, holding a hot cup of coffee. Now imagine there's a being who is extremely smart and extremely malicious, and whose only job is to fool you. He's the one putting the street, the horns and the warm mug into your mind. Even “2 + 3 = 5” might be something he tricked you into believing. How would you prove this isn't happening right now?

Is there anything you can be 100% sure of?

What did philosophers say?

Descartes
He said the demon could fool him about everything except one thing: that he exists and is thinking. To be fooled at all, he has to exist. That's where the famous line comes from: “I think, therefore I am.” He then tried to rebuild the rest of his knowledge on top of it.
David Hume
He thought total doubt like this is something you can entertain alone in your study, but you can't live by it. The moment you get up, talk to people and go about your day, nature beats doubt, and you find yourself believing the chair is there.
Charles S. Peirce (pragmatism)
He criticized Descartes, saying doubt you only perform on paper isn't real doubt. Real doubt shows up when something specific breaks your expectations. That's when you dig in and test.

Why it matters today

You don't need a demon to get fooled. Edited photos, AI-generated videos, texts that look like they came from your bank: they're all playing the same game. Descartes' lesson: before you believe it, ask, “What actually makes me sure?”
