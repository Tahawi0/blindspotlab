Philosopher

Aristotle

Greece, 384–322 BCE

“The First Teacher”: the first to lay out the rules of logic, and the thinker who saw virtue as a middle point between two extremes.

The idea

Aristotle wrote about nearly everything: logic, ethics, politics, biology, poetry. He was the first to set down the rules of the syllogism: if all humans are mortal, and Socrates is a human, then Socrates is mortal. In ethics, he argued that a virtue sits between two vices: courage between cowardice and recklessness, generosity between stinginess and wastefulness. The philosophers of the medieval Arabic-speaking world called him “the First Teacher.”

In real life

One guy puts every group dinner on his credit card to look generous and can't make rent. Another guy has never picked up a check in his life. Neither one is generous. Generosity means giving in the right place, in the right amount.

Use it today

When you notice yourself leaning hard into a trait, ask: am I in the middle, or have I drifted to an extreme? Too much bluntness turns into rudeness. Too much politeness turns into fakeness.

The criticism

Some of his views on nature turned out to be wrong, like the claim that heavier objects fall faster. He also held views about slaves and women that are rightly rejected today. But his method in logic stayed the foundation for centuries.
