Philosopher

Immanuel Kant

Germany (Prussia), 1724–1804

He said the right thing to do is whatever you could accept as a rule for everyone, and that a person is always an end in themselves, never just a means.

The idea

Kant tried to bring the rationalists and the empiricists together. He argued that the mind isn't a blank slate: it organizes experience through built-in frameworks like space, time and causation. In ethics he gave his famous rule, the categorical imperative: act only on a principle you could accept as a universal law. If everyone lied whenever it was convenient, lying itself would stop working, because nobody would believe anybody. He also said: always treat people as ends in themselves, never merely as tools for your goals.

In real life

You're in a hurry, so you want to cut the line at the coffee shop. Ask Kant: if everyone in a hurry cut the line, would there even be a line? No. So it's wrong.

Use it today

Before you do something you're unsure about, ask: if everyone did this, what would the world look like? And ask: am I using this person, or respecting them?

The criticism

His strict rule leads to hard conclusions; he argued that lying is wrong even to a murderer at your door asking where your friend is hiding. Utilitarians find it unreasonable to ignore consequences that completely.
