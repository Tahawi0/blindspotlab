Hypatia of Alexandria

Alexandria, born c. 355–370, died 415 CE

The mathematician and astronomer who taught philosophy in Alexandria, drawing students from far-off places.

The idea

Hypatia was the daughter of Theon, a mathematician in Alexandria. She taught mathematics, astronomy and Neoplatonist philosophy, and was a highly respected figure in the city. Ancient sources say she wrote commentaries on major mathematical works, like Diophantus' Arithmetica and Apollonius' Conics, but none of her writings has survived with certainty. The best source on her is the letters of her student Synesius, which show her teaching students to reason by proof and to build scientific instruments. She was killed in 415 by an angry mob, amid a political power struggle in the city.

In real life

A high school student asks, “When am I ever going to use geometry?” Hypatia's tradition would answer: geometry teaches you not to believe anything until it's proven. You'll need that skill everywhere, even on a used-car lot.

Use it today

Take a fact you believe and try to write out its “proof” step by step, like a geometry problem. Every step has to follow from the one before. Look for the step where you jump.

The criticism

The big problem is how little we actually know about her, so she has become a symbol that every side retells to suit its own story. Many quotes credited to her online were invented centuries later. Even her popular image as “inventor of the astrolabe” isn't supported by evidence.
